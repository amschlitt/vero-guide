About Vero Beach

Vero Beach is a relaxed, low-rise town on Florida's Treasure Coast. It takes its name from a Spanish treasure fleet wrecked offshore by a 1715 hurricane, and coins still wash up today. Building rules here favor “sunrises, not high-rises,” which keeps the shoreline uncrowded. Coastal Living once called it the “Carmel of the Atlantic” for its arts scene and easygoing elegance.

Beaches and nature. The beaches are quiet and windswept, with plenty of wildlife. Manatees can often be seen in the area in winter, and Pelican Island National Wildlife Refuge, the nation's first wildlife refuge, is close by. Sebastian, to the north, is known for its waterways and easy access to nature, and Fellsmere offers open spaces and a rural feel.

Shopping, dining and the arts. Boutiques and galleries cluster near the ocean along Ocean Drive. Downtown has been revitalized, with 14th Avenue at the heart of its restaurants, galleries and events, and a museum and theatre make good rainy-day options.

In the spotlight. Skyscanner named Vero Beach its top trending destination for 2027, and national travel writers have praised its uncrowded beaches and strict low-rise building codes. You are visiting at a good time.

Staying here. Most visitors come to slow down: beach mornings, pool afternoons and dinner outside. Hotels here stay low, so sunrise is yours.
